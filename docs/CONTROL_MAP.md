# The twenty inscriptions

The visible names are original invented Azeroy-inspired labels. They are not authenticated Azeroyska words or translations. Stable IDs S01–S10 and T01–T10 identify their functions in patches and tests. Sliders accept normalized values `x ∈ [0,1]`; switches accept integers `0` or `1`. Every mapping is bounded centrally in `src/Composer.scd` and every source event also clamps frequency, amplitude, pan, drive, tone, reverse, bend, and lifetime.

The front panel is a macro instrument. Moving one control changes related composition and synthesis operations together. “Tone” refers to each voice's actual timbre parameter; in the bell voice it moves from a cheap metallic digital bell toward a breathy FM flute. The DSP stage smooths bits/hold rate at 80 ms, drive at 60 ms, wear at 140 ms, delay time at 150 ms, delay mix at 80 ms, feedback at 120 ms, reverb at 150 ms, width at 80 ms, phone at 90 ms, and master gain at 40 ms. Rhythm, pitch, and envelope edits apply to newly scheduled notes. Complete vocal phrases have their own bounded cutoff, spectral-envelope coefficient, rate, and echo controls rather than behaving as a constant background layer.

## Determinism, recurrence, and limits

`compose(seed, sliders, switches, seconds)` returns finite seconds-based events and scene records. A local Park–Miller PRNG never changes `thisThread.randSeed`. The same seed, controls, and duration yield the same ordered trace. Every emitted voice receives its own deterministic audio-server seed. Exact audio also depends on the installed server version, sample bank, and sample transformation configuration.

The optional fifth `memory` argument carries PRNG state, current/remembered motif and groove indices, root pitch, last three scenes, scene count, vocal countdown, last phrase index, and the previous vocal gate. The result's `memory` can be fed into the next runtime batch. The sixth `singleScene` argument defaults to false; true composes just one variable-length scene, returns its actual duration, and keeps each event's finite voice lifetime so sustained notes and full phrases can overlap the next scene. The seventh `vocalFloor` argument defaults to zero; runtime supplies its remaining actual onset deadline after toggles or reseeding. Main uses these for live scheduling with an ample upper duration bound. This preserves melodic key and history across live control edits. A preset stores controls and a seed, not a recording of an in-progress performance. An offline composition with a different requested duration is a deterministic arrangement of that length, not a promise to replay the preceding performance exactly.

Vocal scheduling uses elapsed seconds rather than a probability checked once per scene. Its countdown is carried across scenes, so fast scene transitions do not create extra sentences. The phrase selector excludes the immediately previous bank index. Turning ZHŪN back on imposes a 3–8 second opening delay while preserving any longer outstanding cooldown. Live playback also enforces at least eight seconds since the last actual utterance, including across toggles and reseeding. The pure composer reserves a conservative six-second lifetime and the sample-preparation stage replaces it with the actual complete clip duration at the chosen rate plus its finite echo tail. Offline compositions leave room for the entire final sentence before their requested endpoint. Live scheduling can let a phrase finish over a scene boundary; interrupted scenes reconcile the cooldown with the time and phrases actually played.

Seven scene families share related pitch material: vacant room, false club, rush, phone corridor, fracture, suspension, and return. Scene transition weights depend on controls and recent history. Immediate/recent repetitions are heavily penalized. Stable four/eight-beat material interleaves with irregular three/five/seven/eleven-beat choices, then UZH adjusts their lengths. Drum cells retain intelligible kick/snare landmarks; melodic variants alter register/articulation before replacing contour. Every scene has a pitched or low rhythmic landmark.

Final safeguards allow at most 96 events in a sliding one-second window and at most 48 simultaneously alive source voices, using each voice's exact finite duration. Emitted amplitudes are ≤0.5, frequencies 20–16000 Hz, bends within ±24 semitones, pan within ±1, and source drives 1–4. The synths free themselves; master limiting remains an additional backstop. Delay time is 0.02–1.5 seconds, feedback ≤0.8, delay mix ≤0.5, reverb ≤0.45, and width 0–1. Asset loading and live scheduling enforce their own limits in Main.
